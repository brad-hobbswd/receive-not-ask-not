Chapter Seven

Learning to Trust What We Cannot See

There are seasons when asking God is easier than trusting Him with the answer. We can come before Jesus with our needs, tell Him what is troubling us, and explain what we hope will happen. But when the days pass and nothing seems to change, we may find ourselves struggling with what comes next. We have asked, we are still waiting, and we cannot see where the situation is leading.

I think one of the hardest parts of faith is learning to live honestly in that space. We want answers because answers help us understand what is happening. We want direction because uncertainty can leave us feeling as though we are standing still. We want reassurance that the prayers we have prayed have not simply disappeared into silence. Yet there are times when we must continue walking without knowing how everything will turn out.

Trust does not require us to pretend that uncertainty feels comfortable. It does not mean we never question, never grieve, or never wish God would make the next step clearer. Trust means that we continue bringing those feelings to Him rather than allowing our uncertainty to become the only voice we hear.

Proverbs 3:5 tells us to trust in the Lord with all our heart and not depend on our own understanding. That instruction is not a command to stop thinking or to ignore what is happening around us. It reminds us that our understanding has limits. We see a portion of the story, while God sees what we cannot. There may be things we understand later that make little sense to us today, and there may be questions that remain unanswered for a long time.

I have learned that we must be careful not to use trust as a way of dismissing another person’s pain. Telling someone to trust God does not remove their need to be heard. Encouraging someone to keep praying does not mean we should overlook the reality of what they are facing. Sometimes people need the freedom to say that they are afraid, disappointed, or unsure before they can explain what trusting God looks like for them.

That is one reason I want these conversations to remain honest. People do not all arrive at faith through the same experiences. Some have prayed and seen answers quickly. Others have waited for years. Some have experienced deep disappointment and are still trying to understand what happened. Their stories deserve to be heard without comparing one person’s faith to another person’s circumstances.

When we cannot see what lies ahead, we may be tempted to stop asking. We might believe that if we cannot understand God’s response, we should no longer bring Him our questions. But we do not have to choose between honesty and faith. We can tell Jesus what we do not understand while continuing to seek His direction.

Perhaps trust begins with the next step rather than the entire journey. We may not know how a relationship will heal, how a difficult season will end, or what tomorrow will bring. But we can ask for wisdom today. We can ask for strength to face what is in front of us. We can ask God to help us recognize the difference between what we know, what we fear, and what we still need to understand.

And when we find ourselves asking the same questions again, we do not have to condemn ourselves for being human. Faith is not measured by how rarely we struggle. Sometimes faith is revealed in our willingness to keep turning toward Jesus even when we are still searching for clarity.

I do not believe we should pretend that every unanswered question will eventually receive the explanation we desire. Some experiences leave us with questions we may carry for a long time. But uncertainty does not have to mean that our conversation with God is over.

We can keep asking. We can keep listening. We can acknowledge what hurts without deciding that the hurt is the whole story.

And perhaps that is part of learning to trust what we cannot see. We continue seeking Jesus, not because we have already figured everything out, but because we know where to bring the questions we cannot answer on our own.
