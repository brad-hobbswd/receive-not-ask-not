Chapter Six

Learning to Ask Again

There comes a point when you have to decide whether disappointment will have the final word in your prayer life. You may have asked God for something that never happened, watched someone you love suffer, or waited so long that you no longer know what to say. Sometimes the hardest part is not believing that God can answer. It is finding the courage to ask Him again after life has taught you to expect disappointment.

I think many people know what it feels like to hold back a prayer. They remember what they once asked for, how much it mattered, and how deeply it hurt when life went another direction. Eventually, silence can feel safer than hope. If you never ask, you never have to face the possibility of another disappointment. Yet silence can also become a place where your heart carries burdens God has invited you to bring to Him.

Jesus taught His disciples that they should always pray and not lose heart. Those words in Luke 18:1 do not promise that every request will be answered on our schedule or in the way we expect. They remind us that disappointment does not have to end our conversation with God.

Learning to ask again is not pretending the past never happened. It is bringing the past with you when you pray. It is being honest about the hurt, the confusion, and the questions that remain. You do not have to manufacture confidence or find perfect words. Sometimes the most honest prayer is simply, “Jesus, I am struggling to ask You again, but I am still here.”

James 4:2 reminds us that there are times when we have not because we ask not. But this verse should never be used to shame someone who has prayed faithfully and is still hurting. Not every unanswered prayer can be explained by a failure to ask. We are learning to approach God honestly, not searching for another reason to blame ourselves.

Perhaps the next step is not asking for everything you lost to be restored exactly as it was. Perhaps it is asking for strength to face tomorrow, wisdom for a decision, peace in a difficult season, or the courage to believe that your life is not finished because one prayer did not unfold as you hoped.

Sometimes we return to prayer with the same request. Sometimes our prayer changes because we have changed. Either way, we can come before Jesus without hiding our disappointment. We can ask, we can listen, and we can remain honest even when we do not understand.

The question is not whether you have ever been disappointed in prayer. Many people have. The question is whether disappointment will keep you from speaking to God again.

And for some of us, learning to ask again begins with admitting how long we have been silent.

When Someone Keeps Asking Despite Being Silenced

The conversation in this chapter's companion story adds an important dimension to learning to ask again. The person I spoke with explained that they could continue asking God questions, yet felt that other people had already labeled them and left little room for them to express what they were feeling. The struggle was not simply whether to pray. It was also whether there was room to ask honest questions without being dismissed.

That distinction matters. Disappointment can make us hesitant to pray, but the response of other people can make it even harder to speak. A person may still be seeking truth while feeling misunderstood by those around them. We should not confuse a difficult question with rebellion, or assume that someone has stopped seeking God because they are trying to understand something that others would rather not discuss.

Learning to ask again includes the courage to bring our questions to Jesus and the humility to listen carefully when someone else is trying to do the same. Read “When You Are Not Allowed to Ask” to hear this conversation in the person's own words.
