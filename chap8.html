Chapter Eight

When Asking for Help Feels Like Weakness

There are people who can pray for everyone around them but struggle to ask someone to pray for them. They know how to encourage others, how to show up when someone is hurting, and how to offer words of hope when life becomes difficult. But when their own lives begin to unravel, they find themselves trying to carry everything alone.

Sometimes it is pride that keeps us silent. Other times, it is the fear of being misunderstood, the embarrassment of admitting that we are struggling, or the belief that we should have enough faith to handle whatever comes our way. We may even convince ourselves that asking for help will make us look weak.

I wonder how many people have suffered quietly because they did not know how to say, “I need someone to pray for me.”

There is a particular kind of loneliness that comes from being the person everyone else depends on. People may see your strength without recognizing your exhaustion. They may come to you for advice without realizing that you are searching for answers yourself. You can become so accustomed to being available for others that you forget you are allowed to need support, too.

Yet there is no shame in admitting that life has become difficult. We are not asked to prove our faith by pretending that we have no needs. Scripture gives us a picture of people who depended on one another, prayed together, and carried burdens together. Galatians 6:2 tells us to bear one another's burdens. That instruction reminds me that we were never meant to treat every struggle as something we must overcome alone.

Asking for help does not mean that we have stopped trusting Jesus. Sometimes asking is an expression of that trust. We recognize that we need wisdom, encouragement, practical assistance, or someone willing to stand with us in prayer. We acknowledge our limitations instead of allowing pride or fear to keep us isolated.

I also believe we need to become better at responding when someone finally does ask. It can take considerable courage for a person to admit that they are not doing well. If we respond with judgment, impatience, or words that minimize their struggle, we may make it harder for them to reach out again. Not every problem can be solved by a conversation, and not every prayer request comes with an easy answer. But we can listen without making someone regret being honest.

There is a difference between telling someone that they should trust God and helping them feel supported while they are learning to trust Him through a difficult season. Sometimes the most meaningful response is not a lengthy explanation. It is being present, listening carefully, and reminding them that their need does not make them a burden.

James 4:2 brings us back to the importance of asking. We often consider that verse in relation to prayer, but it also invites us to examine the ways we respond to our own needs. Are we asking Jesus for help, or are we hiding our struggles because we believe we must appear strong? Are we allowing trusted people to stand beside us, or are we pushing everyone away while hoping someone will notice that we are hurting?

We cannot expect people to understand needs we never communicate, although there will be times when those around us should recognize that someone needs compassion. Learning to ask means becoming willing to speak honestly about what we need, while learning to listen means making room for others to do the same.

You do not have to wait until you reach your breaking point before asking for prayer. You do not have to explain every detail of your situation before admitting that you need support. And you do not have to prove that you can carry everything alone before you are worthy of receiving help.

Sometimes the next step in faith is not another attempt to be stronger. Sometimes it is the humility to say, “I cannot do this alone, and I need help.”

There is no shame in that sentence. There is honesty in it, and honesty gives us somewhere to begin.

Perhaps we have spent so much time asking God to help everyone else that we have forgotten to bring Him our own needs. Perhaps we have become so accustomed to being strong that we no longer know how to let someone stand beside us.

Whatever the reason, we can begin again. We can ask Jesus for what we need, and we can allow trustworthy people to support us along the way.

We were never required to be invulnerable to be faithful. We were invited to come to God honestly, including when we need help.
