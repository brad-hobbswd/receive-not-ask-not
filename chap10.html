Chapter Ten

Keep Asking

We have spent these chapters listening to people who have struggled, waited, prayed, questioned, and continued searching for God in the middle of real life. Their experiences have not all been the same. Some have carried disappointment from prayers that seemed unanswered. Others have faced sickness, loss, loneliness, or the responsibility of trying to hold everything together when life changed unexpectedly.

Some knew exactly what they wanted to ask God for. Others reached a point where they could no longer find the words.

But there is something that connects these stories. Each one reminds us that prayer is not separate from the lives we are living. We bring our grief, our uncertainty, our needs, our hopes, and our questions before Jesus. We come with what we understand and what we cannot yet explain.

And sometimes, we have to learn how to ask again.

James 4:2 gives us the words that inspired this book: “Ye have not, because ye ask not” (AMPC).

That verse challenges us to consider whether there are things we have never brought before God. Have we allowed fear to keep us silent? Have past disappointments made us reluctant to hope again? Have we become so accustomed to carrying our burdens alone that we no longer know how to ask for help?

Those are questions worth considering, but they should never become another weapon we use against ourselves or others. People can pray faithfully and still experience pain. They can ask repeatedly and still wait. They can trust God and still struggle to understand what has happened. We must not assume that every disappointment is the result of someone failing to pray correctly or ask enough times.

The invitation to ask is not a promise that we control God's response. It is an invitation to bring our needs honestly before Him.

Throughout this book, I have been reminded that there is no single way to experience the struggle of prayer. Joey's story showed how difficult it can be to ask when you are accustomed to being strong for others. Justin's experience revealed the anger and hurt that can remain after a devastating injustice. Ashley's story carried the weight of difficult relationships and the complicated decisions involved in trying to move forward. Sister Pauline reminded us that some prayers take years and that life does not always unfold the way we expect.

We also heard from someone who continued asking questions while feeling labeled and silenced by others. Jackie shared her ongoing struggle with sickness and her deep love for the Lord and her church family. My dad's experience reminded me that grief can leave people facing responsibilities they never expected to carry. And my Mamaw Anna showed us what it meant to keep praying when she did not know what to do.

These stories do not offer one simple explanation for every hardship. They are not meant to. They remind us that behind every prayer request is a person whose experience deserves to be heard.

I have learned that asking God for something requires honesty. We must be willing to acknowledge what we need, even when the admission makes us uncomfortable. We must also be willing to recognize that sometimes the help we need includes practical support, wise counsel, medical care, or the willingness to let another person stand beside us.

Prayer does not require us to deny our circumstances. It gives us a place to bring them.

There may be something you have been afraid to ask God for. Perhaps you have convinced yourself that it is too late, that you have already asked too many times, or that your disappointment means you should stop hoping. Maybe you have been praying for someone else for so long that you have forgotten your own needs. Or perhaps you are in a season when you cannot even explain what you need anymore.

You can begin where you are.

You can tell Jesus the truth about what hurts. You can ask Him for wisdom when you do not know what to do. You can ask for strength when you are tired, courage when you are afraid, and direction when the next step is unclear. You can bring Him questions that have remained unanswered and concerns that you have struggled to express.

You do not have to pretend that you understand everything before you pray.

And if you have been disappointed, you do not have to pretend that the disappointment never happened. You can bring that to Him, too.

I also hope we learn to listen differently to one another. When someone shares a prayer request, may we resist the urge to offer an immediate explanation for their suffering. When someone asks a difficult question, may we listen before deciding what their question means. When someone is struggling, may we remember that a person can have faith and still need help.

We cannot promise another person that everything will turn out exactly as they hope. We cannot explain every loss or guarantee that every question will receive an answer in the time we desire. But we can pray with one another, offer practical support, and create room for honest conversations about faith and life.

Perhaps that is part of what it means to live out the invitation to ask. We ask God honestly, and we become people who are willing to hear the honest requests of others.

As I come to the end of this book, I keep returning to a simple truth: you do not have to stop seeking Jesus because you do not understand what is happening. You do not have to silence your questions because someone else is uncomfortable hearing them. And you do not have to wait until your circumstances are perfect before you come to God.

Keep asking when you know what you need.

Keep asking when you are still trying to understand what you need.

Keep asking when the wait has been longer than you expected.

And when you cannot find the words, begin with the truth that you need Him.

I think about my Mamaw Anna and how she would keep right on praying until she heard from heaven. I think about Sister Pauline and the years of prayer she carried through a long life. I think about Jackie, who continues to trust the Lord while facing her health challenges. I think about every person who has shared a part of their story and trusted me to listen.

Their lives remind me that prayer is not reserved for people who have everything figured out. It belongs in the middle of real life, where the questions are sometimes difficult, the answers are not always immediate, and the need for God remains.

This book began with a question about why we sometimes do not ask. I hope it leaves you willing to consider what you have been holding back, what you need to bring before Jesus, and whether there is a conversation with Him that you need to begin again.

You may not know how the story will end. You may not know what tomorrow will bring. But you can still come to Him today.

Keep asking. Keep seeking Jesus. And do not be afraid to bring Him the truth about where you are.
