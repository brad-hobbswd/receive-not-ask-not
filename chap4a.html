# Chapter 4A ## When All You Want Is for Life to Change *Bradley Hobbs speaks with his sister Ashley about relationships, difficult decisions, and finding a way forward.* Sometimes life does not unfold the way we imagined it would. We enter relationships hoping for love, stability, and a future we can build together. We make decisions based on what we know at the time, sometimes believing things will work out even when there are signs that something is wrong. For some people, the difficult part is not one relationship or one painful experience. It is finding themselves in one difficult situation after another and trying to understand how to move forward without losing themselves along the way. When I thought about the question of asking God to change a situation or another person, I wanted to explore it with my sister, Ashley. Her life has included several relationships, children, difficult transitions, and decisions that affected not only her but her family as well. **Brad:** Have you ever asked God to change a situation or another person, only to realize later that there was something within yourself you also needed to face? **Ashley:** When I look back over my life and the relationships I have been in, I can see how many different situations I have had to work through. I have children with different fathers, and I have experienced relationships that did not turn out the way I hoped they would. One relationship involved a man who was hiding hard drugs and being unfaithful. Another involved someone whose faith and personal insecurities were part of the relationship, and things eventually reached a point where I felt I needed to leave. **Brad:** What happened when you decided you needed to get out of that situation? **Ashley:** I left and stayed with a friend for a long time before eventually moving to Kentucky. Leaving was a major transition in my life. My health was deteriorating, and my mental health was suffering, too. I was trying to get out of a difficult situation while dealing with everything that came with that decision. **Brad:** You also had to make difficult decisions involving your children. How did that part of your life unfold? **Ashley:** My children were at different stages of their lives. One was nineteen, another was almost eighteen and autistic, and my youngest was ten. I left my youngest with his father, and I made arrangements for the children through legal documentation that I had notarized. It was a difficult period, and there were a lot of things happening at once. I was trying to make decisions while dealing with my own health and emotional struggles. **Brad:** What has life looked like since moving to Kentucky? **Ashley:** I eventually went through a divorce and later entered another relationship. My current partner is living under restrictions that include an ankle monitor and five years of probation. Despite those circumstances, I feel safe with him. I have a job and a vehicle now, and I am saving toward buying a home with him on the land where I live. My life has not been perfect, but I am working toward something more stable. ## What This Conversation Left Me Thinking About Listening to Ashley's story reminded me that the desire for life to change can come from many different places. Sometimes people are looking for love. Sometimes they are looking for stability. Sometimes they are simply trying to find a way out of circumstances that have become too difficult to manage. When we look at another person's life from the outside, it can be easy to focus on the decisions they made. We may see one relationship after another and wonder why they continued searching for something different. But a person's life cannot be fully understood through a list of relationships or mistakes. There are circumstances, responsibilities, vulnerabilities, and personal struggles that others may never see. Ashley experienced serious challenges in her relationships, and her physical and mental health suffered during one of those periods. Her decision to leave, relocate, and make arrangements for her children marked a significant transition. Those circumstances deserve to be considered with care rather than reduced to a simple judgment about what she should have done. Her current circumstances are also part of her story. She has employment, transportation, and a goal of saving for a home. She says she feels safe in her current relationship. Those are important details about where she is today, even as the restrictions affecting her partner remain part of their circumstances. James 4:3 asks us to consider the motives behind our requests. That passage invites personal reflection, but it should never become an excuse to blame someone for every hardship they have experienced. We cannot assume that Ashley's relationships unfolded as they did because she failed to ask God for the right thing or because there was something inherently wrong with her. Instead, her story leaves us with a more careful question: When we desperately want our circumstances to change, how do we recognize what we need, what is safe for us, and what decisions will help us move toward a more stable future? Prayer can be part of that process, but so can practical decisions, support from trusted people, attention to our health, and an honest assessment of our circumstances. Sometimes asking God for change also means being willing to face the decisions before us. That does not mean we control every outcome or that we can prevent every painful experience. It means we can seek wisdom as we decide what to do next. Ashley's story is still unfolding. She is working, saving, and pursuing a home of her own. Her future is not something I can predict, and I do not need to pretend that every question has already been answered. What matters is that her experience reminds us how complicated real lives can be. Behind every decision is a person trying to find a way forward, and behind every story is more than what we can see from the outside.
