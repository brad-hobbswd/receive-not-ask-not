When the Answer Takes Years

Some people speak about prayer from what they have recently experienced. Others speak from a lifetime of asking, waiting, and discovering that life does not always unfold the way they imagined. When you sit with someone who has walked with God for decades, you begin to realize that the story of prayer is often much longer than the moment when the request was first made.

Today, I had a conversation with Sister Pauline, a woman who is ninety one years old. As we talked about prayer and the things we ask God for, she reflected on how many years she had spent praying for different things in her life.

Some prayers were answered quickly. Others took years upon years. And some things did not turn out the way she wanted them to. Yet when she looked back, she recognized that things had unfolded according to God's way rather than her own expectations.

That perspective stayed with me.

Bradley: Sister Pauline, when you look back over your life, have there been things you prayed for that took a long time to happen?

Sister Pauline: Yes. I have prayed for many things over the years. Some things did not turn out the way I wanted, but I came to understand that God had His way of doing things. Some prayers did not take long to be answered, while others took years upon years.

Bradley: After all those years, how do you look at those prayers now?

Sister Pauline: When I look back, I can see that things did not always happen the way I expected. I had to learn that God's way was not always my way. Some things took a long time, and other things turned out differently from what I had prayed for.

What I Took Away From Our Conversation

As I listened to Sister Pauline, I found myself thinking about the difference between the way we experience waiting when we are young and the way we understand it after many years of living.

When we first ask God for something, we often have an idea of how the answer should look. We imagine the outcome, the timing, and the relief we will feel when the situation finally changes. When it does not happen that way, we may struggle to understand what the delay means.

But Sister Pauline has lived long enough to look back across many seasons of prayer. At ninety one, she can speak about requests that came quickly, requests that took years, and things that never unfolded according to her wishes. Her perspective reminds me that a lifetime of faith is not built around receiving every answer on our preferred timetable.

What struck me most was not simply her age, but the way she continues to express her faith. I saw a woman whose life still reflected her devotion to Jesus, through prayer, speaking in tongues, and prophetic expression. Her faith was not merely a memory of what she once believed. It was something she continued to live.

James 4:2 asks us to consider the importance of asking God, but Sister Pauline's story reminds us that asking is only part of the journey. We must also face the reality that we cannot always determine when an answer will come or whether our lives will unfold as we envisioned.

I do not believe that every disappointment can be explained by saying that God simply wanted things to happen differently. There are experiences in life that remain painful and difficult to understand. But we can learn from someone who has continued walking with God through changing circumstances without measuring His faithfulness solely by whether every request was fulfilled as expected.

Perhaps this is one of the things we lose sight of when we become discouraged. We focus so intensely on the prayer that has not been answered that we overlook the years we have already lived, the strength we have received, and the ways our understanding has changed along the way.

Sister Pauline's story reminded me that prayer is not limited to the moment when we ask or the moment when we receive. It is part of a relationship with God that continues through the waiting, the unexpected turns, and the seasons when we must reconsider what we thought would happen.

Some prayers may be answered quickly. Others may remain with us for years. And sometimes, when we look back, we discover that our lives did not follow the path we had planned.

Yet the invitation to seek God remains.

At ninety one, Sister Pauline continues to pray and express her faith in Jesus. That, in itself, gave me something to reflect on. Her story reminds me that our relationship with God is not measured by how quickly every circumstance changes, but by the life of faith we continue to live as the years unfold.

Perhaps learning to ask is only the beginning. Learning to trust God through a lifetime of answers, delays, and disappointments is part of the journey, too.
