Real conversation

When You Are Not Allowed to Ask

Brad: Have you ever felt like people had already decided who you were before they were willing to hear what you had to say?

Anonymous: I am someone people label as something that should not even have to be named, but it is obvious who they are talking about. That is not even the real issue. I can still ask God questions, but it feels like nobody will let me feel what I feel or say what I need to say.

Brad: What do you mean when you say nobody will let you feel?

Anonymous: I mean that when I try to express something, it can feel like there is no room for it. It is as if I am supposed to accept what others say without being allowed to question it. I keep thinking there is something wrong with that.

Brad: Does that stop you from asking God questions?

Anonymous: No. I keep asking. I keep trying to get beneath the surface of what is happening. I feel like something is coming, something that has not fully come to the surface yet.

A Reflection

There is a difference between asking God a question and being given permission by people to ask it. Sometimes people attach a label to someone so firmly that they no longer listen to the person behind it. Once that happens, even an honest question can be treated as a challenge, and an expression of pain can be mistaken for rebellion.

What stayed with me about this conversation was the person’s determination to keep asking God questions. They felt that something was wrong with being denied the freedom to express what they felt, yet they had not stopped reaching for God. Their questions remained, and so did their desire to understand what was happening beneath the surface.

I believe there is room in our walk with Jesus to be honest about what we do not understand. Scripture gives us examples of people who cried out to God, wrestled with difficult circumstances, and asked questions from the depths of their hearts. Honest questions do not automatically mean someone has abandoned faith. Sometimes they reveal a person who is still searching for truth.

I also recognize that I cannot decide what this person’s experience means before hearing everything they have to say. Their story deserves room to unfold without forcing it into a conclusion. Not every question can be answered immediately, and not every feeling can be resolved in one conversation.

But I keep coming back to this thought: Why are we sometimes more comfortable labeling a person than listening to what they are trying to tell us?

Perhaps the very thing we are trying to keep beneath the surface is the thing we need to be willing to face honestly. And perhaps learning to ask again means believing that we can bring our questions to Jesus, even when others would rather we remain silent.

When You Are Not Allowed to Ask

There was something about this conversation that stayed with me. This person described being labeled by others, with the label itself being something they did not even want to name. Yet they made it clear that the label was not the real issue. What troubled them was the feeling that they could still ask God questions, but they were not being allowed to express what they felt or say what was on their heart.

That distinction matters.

Sometimes people become so focused on what they believe someone is that they stop listening to what that person is actually saying. A label can become a substitute for understanding. Once a person has been placed into a category, their questions may be dismissed before anyone takes the time to hear them. Their feelings may be judged before anyone asks why they feel that way.

I cannot speak for every person who has experienced this, and I will not pretend to know everything this individual was carrying. What I can do is listen to what they shared and recognize the tension in their words. They were not saying that they had stopped asking God questions. They were saying that people were making it difficult for them to express what they felt, even while they continued seeking God.

That raises a question worth considering. When did asking an honest question become something we were afraid to allow?

There is a difference between challenging someone simply to create conflict and asking a sincere question because something does not make sense. There is also a difference between refusing correction and needing room to explain what you are experiencing. We should be able to recognize those differences without automatically assuming the worst about someone.

Scripture does not portray every person who comes before God as having everything figured out. The Psalms contain cries of confusion, sorrow, frustration, and longing. Job wrestled with painful questions. Even the disciples asked Jesus questions when they did not understand what He was teaching them. Their questions became part of their journey toward understanding.

We should be careful, then, about treating every question as evidence of a rebellious heart. Sometimes a question is the beginning of a person confronting something they have struggled to understand for a long time. Sometimes it is the first honest expression of a hurt they have kept hidden. And sometimes the person asking the question is still trying to find the words for something they have not fully understood themselves.

What struck me most was this person’s statement that they kept asking, trying to break through the surface, because they felt something was coming to the surface. I do not know exactly what that will mean for them, and I do not want to decide the outcome of their story for them. But their words suggest that they are not content to remain at the level of appearances. They want to understand what lies beneath them.

That process can be uncomfortable. It can require people to examine assumptions they have held for years. It can reveal questions that have been avoided and feelings that have not been given room to be expressed. Yet avoiding those things does not necessarily make them disappear. Sometimes silence only leaves a person carrying the questions alone.

This is where the message of You Have Not Because You Ask Not reaches beyond the words we speak in prayer. It invites us to consider whether we have become afraid to ask at all. We may still believe in God, still pray, and still desire truth, while struggling with the fear of what might happen if we say what we genuinely feel.

But we do not have to hide our questions from Jesus. We can bring Him our confusion without pretending to understand everything. We can ask Him to expose what is true, correct what is wrong, and help us recognize what we have not yet seen clearly. Asking does not mean we already know the answer. It means we are willing to seek it.

I also believe we need to consider our responsibility toward one another. If someone comes to us with a sincere question, do we listen before we judge? Do we allow room for an honest conversation, or have we already decided what their words must mean? Are we willing to hear a person whose experience challenges our assumptions?

We cannot promise that every conversation will end in agreement. We cannot promise that every question will receive an immediate answer. But we can choose not to silence someone simply because their questions make us uncomfortable.

This person has reminded me that asking God questions and struggling with how people respond are not necessarily the same thing. Someone can continue seeking Jesus while feeling misunderstood by others. Someone can want truth while still needing room to express pain, uncertainty, or frustration. We should not assume that their struggle tells us everything about their relationship with God.

Perhaps something really is coming to the surface. Perhaps the person is beginning to find words for something that has been difficult to express. Whatever the outcome, their story deserves to be heard without our rushing to label it, explain it away, or force it into a conclusion.

Because sometimes the first step toward understanding is not having the answer.

Sometimes it is finally being able to ask the question.

And sometimes, when everyone around you seems to have already decided who you are, the courage to keep asking Jesus for truth becomes an important part of your journey.

You can be willing to hear the truth and still need room to ask the question.
