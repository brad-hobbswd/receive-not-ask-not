Chapter 6A When You Are Not Allowed to Ask Brad: Have you ever felt like people had already decided who you were before they were willing to hear what you had to say? Anonymous: I am someone people label as something that should not even have to be named, but it is obvious who they are talking about. That is not even the real issue. I can still ask God questions, but it feels like nobody will let me feel what I feel or say what I need to say. Brad: What do you mean when you say nobody will let you feel? Anonymous: I mean that when I try to express something, it can feel like there is no room for it. It is as if I am supposed to accept what others say without being allowed to question it. I keep thinking there is something wrong with that. Brad: Does that stop you from asking God questions? Anonymous: No. I keep asking. I keep trying to get beneath the surface of what is happening. I feel like something is coming, something that has not fully come to the surface yet. A Reflection There is a difference between asking God a question and being given permission by people to ask it. Sometimes people attach a label to someone so firmly that they no longer listen to the person behind it. Once that happens, even an honest question can be treated as a challenge, and an expression of pain can be mistaken for rebellion. What stayed with me about this conversation was the person’s determination to keep asking God questions. They felt that something was wrong with being denied the freedom to express what they felt, yet they had not stopped reaching for God. Their questions remained, and so did their desire to understand what was happening beneath the surface. I believe there is room in our walk with Jesus to be honest about what we do not understand. Scripture gives us examples of people who cried out to God, wrestled with difficult circumstances, and asked questions from the depths of their hearts. Honest questions do not automatically mean someone has abandoned faith. Sometimes they reveal a person who is still searching for truth. I also recognize that I cannot decide what this person’s experience means before hearing everything they have to say. Their story deserves room to unfold without forcing it into a conclusion. Not every question can be answered immediately, and not every feeling can be resolved in one conversation. But I keep coming back to this thought: Why are we sometimes more comfortable labeling a person than listening to what they are trying to tell us? Perhaps the very thing we are trying to keep beneath the surface is the thing we need to be willing to face honestly. And perhaps learning to ask again means believing that we can bring our questions to Jesus, even when others would rather we remain silent.
