About the author

Bradley Hobbs

Bradley Hobbs is a Christian author, biblical teacher, and educator whose writing brings Scripture into conversation with the questions, struggles, and experiences of everyday life.

His work is shaped by a simple conviction: faith should have room for honest questions. People do not come to God from identical circumstances, and they do not all experience prayer in the same way. Some have stories of answers that came quickly. Others know what it means to wait, to wrestle with disappointment, or to keep believing while trying to understand what is happening. Bradley writes with a desire to make room for those experiences without reducing them to easy explanations.

In You Have Not Because You Ask Not, he explores the relationship between prayer, asking, waiting, and receiving. Through biblical reflection and personal conversations, the book listens to people whose experiences are different from one another. Their stories are not treated as formulas or proof that every situation will have the same outcome. Instead, they invite readers to consider their own relationship with God, the things they have been afraid to ask, and the support they may need along the way.

Bradley's approach to writing is rooted in listening. He believes that people's stories deserve to be heard with care, especially when those stories include grief, uncertainty, disappointment, or questions that are difficult to express. He seeks to connect those real experiences with the truth of Scripture while allowing the conversation to remain honest. Not every chapter ends with every question resolved, because real faith is often lived while questions are still being worked through.

Alongside his writing and biblical teaching, Bradley has a background in education and holds a bachelor's degree in Computer Information Technology and Business Management. His experience as an educator has strengthened his appreciation for clear communication, thoughtful instruction, and meeting people where they are. Whether he is developing a Bible study, writing a devotional, or working on a longer teaching project, his aim is to make meaningful ideas understandable and useful beyond the page.

Bradley hopes his work will encourage readers who have felt overlooked, misunderstood, discouraged, or uncertain about how to approach God with what is on their hearts. He does not believe that asking honest questions means a person has abandoned faith. Sometimes the willingness to bring those questions before Jesus is itself an important step in the journey.

“Faith should never require us to pretend that we have no questions. We can bring our questions to Jesus and continue seeking Him while we learn.”

You Have Not Because You Ask Not is an invitation to reflect, to listen, and to rediscover the importance of asking. Bradley's hope is that readers will recognize that they are not alone in their questions and that their conversations with God do not have to end simply because the answers have not yet come.
