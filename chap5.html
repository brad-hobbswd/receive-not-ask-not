# Chapter Five ## When the Answer Never Comes There are prayers we carry for so long that they become part of our everyday lives. We wake up with them, think about them while we work, and return to them when the day becomes quiet. Sometimes we tell other people what we are praying for. Other times, we keep those requests to ourselves because we are tired of explaining why we are still waiting. At first, we may pray with expectation. We believe something can change, and we look for signs that our circumstances are beginning to move in the direction we hoped. But as time passes, we may find ourselves asking different questions. Why has nothing changed? Did I misunderstand what I was praying for? Should I keep asking, or do I need to learn how to live with the possibility that things may never turn out the way I imagined? These questions can be difficult because we do not always know how to reconcile our expectations with our experiences. We hear stories about answered prayers and celebrate when someone receives the outcome they hoped for. Yet there are people who have prayed for years and are still waiting. Their stories matter, too. I think we need to make room for those experiences without offering explanations we cannot support. We cannot always know why a particular prayer seems unanswered. We cannot assume that someone lacked faith, failed to pray correctly, or did something to deserve the outcome they received. Scripture itself gives us room to acknowledge the difficulty of waiting. In Psalm 13, David asks how long he will feel forgotten and how long sorrow will remain in his heart. His words remind us that bringing difficult questions before God is not the same as abandoning faith. Sometimes we continue praying because we believe the circumstances can change. Sometimes we pray because we do not know what else to do with the burden we are carrying. And sometimes we struggle to pray because the disappointment has become difficult to express. Perhaps faith is not always found in knowing how a story will end. Perhaps it can also be found in being honest about where we are while continuing to seek God. I want to hear from people who have lived with unanswered questions. Not just people who eventually received what they prayed for, but people who are still waiting, people whose circumstances changed in unexpected ways, and people who have had to reconsider what they believe about prayer. Their experiences may not give us a single explanation for why answers sometimes seem absent. But they may help us understand one another better and remind us that no one should have to pretend that waiting is easy. Sometimes the hardest prayer is the one we have repeated so many times that we no longer know what to say. And sometimes the most honest thing we can do is admit that we are still waiting.
