Chapter One

Why We Don't Ask

There is a difference between needing something and being willing to ask for it. Most of us know what it feels like to need help, yet admitting that need can be much harder than carrying it ourselves. Sometimes pride gets in the way. Sometimes fear holds us back. And sometimes we have been disappointed so many times that we begin to wonder whether asking will make any difference.

I wonder how often this happens in our relationship with God.

We talk about trusting Him, believing Him, and depending on Him, but there may be areas of our lives we have never honestly brought before Him. We pray for others while keeping our own struggles hidden. We ask God to strengthen our families while quietly struggling with our own fears. We encourage people to keep believing even when we are unsure how to keep believing ourselves.

Why is it sometimes easier to talk about prayer than it is to be honest about what we need?

James 4:2 presents a question that deserves our attention: “Ye have not, because ye ask not”. These words challenge us to consider whether we have neglected the invitation to bring our needs before God. Yet they also lead us to examine the reasons we hesitate to ask.

Perhaps some of us were taught that asking for personal needs was selfish. Perhaps we believe that God already knows everything, so there is no reason to tell Him what He already understands. Others may have learned to depend on themselves because the people they once trusted were never there when they needed help.

Experiences like these can influence the way we approach God, even when we do not realize it.

Imagine someone who has spent years being the dependable person in every room. When a family member struggles, they show up. When a friend needs encouragement, they listen. When a crisis arrives, they find a way to keep moving. Yet when their own life begins to fall apart, they struggle to admit that they need someone to help carry the weight.

Perhaps asking God feels just as difficult because they have become accustomed to being the person who handles everything.

We cannot assume that everyone who struggles to pray has the same reason for doing so. That is why I want to hear from people themselves. I want to understand what they believe about prayer, what they have experienced, and what has shaped their willingness to ask God for help.

There is something important about listening before we offer an answer. We may discover that what looks like a lack of faith is actually disappointment. What appears to be independence may be the result of years of having to manage everything alone. And what sounds like silence toward God may come from questions a person has never felt safe enough to express.

Perhaps the first step is not to tell people that they need to pray more. Perhaps it is to understand why they have struggled to ask in the first place.

After all, we cannot understand the significance of asking until we are willing to examine what has kept us from doing it.
