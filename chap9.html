# Chapter Nine ## When You Don't Know What to Ask For There are moments when you know you need God, but you do not know what to ask Him for. You know something is wrong, you know you are tired, and you know you cannot keep carrying everything the way you have been. But when you finally try to pray, the words do not come. You may have spent so much time asking for one thing that you no longer know what to ask for when life takes a different direction. Perhaps you prayed for a relationship to be restored, but it ended. Perhaps you prayed for healing, but the struggle continued. Perhaps you asked God to open a door, only to find yourself standing in a place you never expected to be. What do you pray when the future you imagined no longer looks possible? Sometimes we become so focused on a particular outcome that we struggle to imagine anything beyond it. We know what we want God to do, and we may have spent months or years asking Him to do it. When circumstances change, we are left trying to understand not only what happened but also what we should ask for now. I think we need to give ourselves permission to admit that we do not always know. Romans 8:26 speaks about our weakness and tells us that we do not always know what we ought to pray for. That passage is a reminder that our limitations do not prevent us from coming to God. We do not have to arrive with a carefully organized prayer or a complete understanding of our circumstances. Sometimes the most honest prayer is, “Jesus, I do not even know what to ask You for anymore.” There is no need to dress that sentence up. It expresses uncertainty, exhaustion, and the recognition that we need help beyond what we can explain. It does not mean that we have abandoned faith. It means we have reached a place where our own understanding is not enough to guide us. We can become so concerned about praying correctly that we forget prayer is also where we bring our confusion. We may hear other people pray confidently and wonder why we cannot find the same words. We may believe that our prayers are somehow inadequate because we cannot explain what we need. But God is not limited by our ability to describe our situation perfectly. We can come before Him with questions, tears, silence, and unfinished thoughts. We can ask for wisdom when we do not know what decision to make. We can ask for strength when we cannot see how to continue. We can ask for peace while we are still trying to understand what has happened. And when we cannot identify what we need, we can begin there. Perhaps you do not know whether you need a new beginning or the courage to let go of the past. Perhaps you are unsure whether to keep waiting or begin moving forward. Perhaps you have spent so long trying to survive that you have not had the opportunity to consider what healing might look like for you. You do not have to answer every question before you pray. Sometimes our first request needs to be for wisdom. Sometimes it is for direction. Sometimes we need the courage to face a truth we have been avoiding, or the strength to accept that another person's decisions are beyond our control. There are also times when what we need most is practical help, a safe person to talk to, or support from someone who understands what we are facing. Prayer does not require us to ignore those needs. We can seek Jesus while also reaching out for help, making difficult decisions, and taking responsible steps toward change. This matters because we should never use faith to make people feel guilty for being uncertain. A person who does not know what to ask for is not automatically a person who lacks faith. Sometimes uncertainty comes from grief. Sometimes it follows disappointment. Sometimes life has changed so much that the old prayers no longer seem to fit the present circumstances. We need room to be honest about that. James 4:2 brings us back to the importance of asking, but asking is not always as simple as naming a specific outcome. There are times when we know exactly what we want. There are other times when we can only acknowledge that we need God to help us understand the next step. We may begin by asking Him to search our hearts, give us wisdom, show us what we have not seen, and help us recognize what is within our responsibility. We can ask for the courage to move forward without pretending we know how the entire journey will unfold. I have come to appreciate that not every prayer needs to begin with a clear plan. Sometimes prayer begins with an admission: I am confused. I am hurting. I do not understand. I need help. Those words can become the beginning of a more honest conversation with Jesus. If you do not know what to ask God for today, you can begin by telling Him exactly that. You do not have to manufacture certainty. You do not have to repeat words that do not reflect what is happening in your heart. You can come as you are and ask Him to help you find your way. Perhaps clarity will come one step at a time. Perhaps the first answer you recognize will not be the answer you expected. And perhaps some questions will remain while you continue learning how to walk through the season you are in. You can still pray. You can still ask. And even when you do not know what the next request should be, you can begin with the one truth you already recognize: you need God, and you are willing to come to Him.
