# You Have Not Because You Ask Not ### By Bradley Hobbs ## Introduction: Have You Ever Wondered Why? Have you ever prayed for something and wondered why it never happened? Have you ever watched someone else receive what you had been asking God for and quietly questioned why your own prayers seemed to go unanswered? Perhaps you have prayed until you no longer knew what else to say. Perhaps you stopped asking because disappointment made it difficult to believe that anything would change. Or perhaps you have spent so much time taking care of everyone else that you have forgotten how to bring your own needs before God. James 4:2 gives us a statement that sounds simple: “Ye have not, because ye ask not”. But what does that really mean when life becomes complicated? What does it mean to someone who has prayed faithfully and is still waiting? What does it mean to someone who has never felt comfortable asking God for anything? And what about the person who has stopped praying altogether because of something they experienced in life or in church? These are questions worth exploring, not because every question has an easy answer, but because our experiences often reveal things about ourselves, our expectations, and our relationship with God that we might otherwise overlook. I want to explore these questions through real conversations with people from different backgrounds and experiences. Some may have strong faith. Others may carry doubts they have never felt comfortable expressing. Some may have stories of answered prayer, while others may still be trying to understand what happened when the answer never came. I do not expect every story to end the same way, and I do not intend to force every experience into a neat conclusion. I want to listen, ask questions, consider what Scripture says, and reflect honestly on what these conversations reveal. Perhaps somewhere in these pages, you will recognize something of your own story. Perhaps you will discover a question you have never considered or find words for something you have struggled to explain. Because the question is not simply whether we have asked God for something. It is whether we understand what it means to ask, what we expect when we ask, and what happens to our faith when the answer is different from what we imagined. Maybe it is time we started talking about it.
