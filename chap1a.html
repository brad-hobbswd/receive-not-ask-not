# Chapter 1A ## The Things We Find Difficult to Ask For *An honest conversation with Joey about prayer, personal struggles, and learning to ask.* **Brad:** Joey, let me ask you something. Have you ever needed God to do something in your life but struggled to ask Him for it? **Joey:** Yes, definitely. There have been times when I knew I needed God, but I honestly didn't know how to approach Him about what I was going through. It wasn't that I didn't believe in prayer. Sometimes I just didn't know what to say. **Brad:** What do you think made it so difficult? Were you afraid of what might happen, or was it more about being disappointed if things didn't change? **Joey:** I think it was a little of both. Sometimes I was afraid that nothing would change. Other times, I felt like I should have been strong enough to handle things myself. I was used to being there for other people, and admitting that I needed help wasn't easy. **Brad:** I think a lot of people can relate to that. We can be the ones everyone else comes to when they need something, but when we need help, we don't always know how to admit it. Do you think you ever confused being strong with not needing help? **Joey:** Probably. I had learned how to keep going even when things were difficult. People saw me as someone who could handle things, so I kept trying to live up to that. Eventually, I think I started believing that I had to handle everything on my own. **Brad:** Did that carry over into your relationship with God, too? **Joey:** It did. I would pray, but sometimes my prayers were more about getting through the day than actually telling God what was happening inside me. I would ask Him to help other people, but when it came to my own struggles, I didn't always know how to be honest. **Brad:** Why do you think it was easier to pray for other people than it was to pray for yourself? **Joey:** When someone else was hurting, I could see their need. I wanted God to help them. But when I was the one hurting, I would question whether my needs were important enough to bring to Him. I knew God cared about people, but sometimes I struggled to believe that He cared about what I was carrying. **Brad:** That's something worth thinking about. You believed God could help, but you weren't always sure whether you should ask Him to help you. **Joey:** Exactly. Looking back, I can see how different those two things are. I never stopped believing that God had power. I just wasn't always sure how to approach Him with my own needs. **Brad:** When you hear James 4:2, which says, “Ye have not, because ye ask not” (AMPC), what does that make you think about? **Joey:** It makes me think about the things I never brought to God because I had already convinced myself that I needed to deal with them alone. I can't say that every situation would have turned out differently if I had prayed differently, but I can recognize that there were times when I kept things to myself instead of being honest with God. **Brad:** Do you think people sometimes stop asking because they've asked before and been disappointed? **Joey:** Absolutely. When you've prayed about something for a long time and nothing seems to change, it can become difficult to pray about it again. You start wondering whether you should keep asking or whether you should just accept that things may never be different. **Brad:** What would you say to someone who is in that place right now, someone who has prayed and prayed but feels like they don't have the strength to keep asking? **Joey:** I would tell them that struggling to pray doesn't necessarily mean they've stopped believing. Sometimes they're tired. Sometimes they're hurt. Sometimes they simply don't know what to say anymore. I think they need to be honest about that instead of pretending everything is fine. **Brad:** If you could go back and tell your younger self something about prayer, what would you say? **Joey:** I would tell myself that I don't have to wait until I have everything figured out before I talk to God. I don't have to make my prayers sound perfect, and I don't have to pretend that I'm stronger than I feel. I can come to Him honestly, even when I'm confused or afraid. ## What I Took Away From Our Conversation As I listened to Joey, I kept coming back to something that is easy to overlook. Sometimes the difficulty isn't believing that God has the power to help us. Sometimes it is learning to bring our own needs before Him when we have spent so much of our lives trying to be strong for everyone else. There is a difference between believing that God can help and allowing ourselves to admit that we need His help. We can encourage others to pray, remind them to trust God, and tell them to keep believing, yet struggle to find the words when we are the ones who need encouragement. Joey's answers also reminded me that we cannot assume we know why someone struggles with prayer. What looks like a lack of faith may be disappointment. What looks like independence may come from years of feeling responsible for everything. And what sounds like silence may be a person who no longer knows how to express what is happening inside. James 4:2 challenges us to consider the importance of asking God, but it does not mean that every unanswered prayer is our fault or that everything we request will happen exactly as we hope. The surrounding verses also ask us to examine our motives. Prayer is not about controlling God or guaranteeing an outcome. It is about bringing our needs, desires, and concerns before Him honestly. What stayed with me most was Joey's recognition that he did not have to wait until he had everything figured out before talking to God. Perhaps some of us have made prayer more complicated than it needs to be. We think we must find the right words, reach the right spiritual condition, or become strong enough to handle our circumstances before we approach God. Yet the very thing we are trying to overcome may be the thing we need to bring before Him. I wonder how many people are carrying things they have never learned to put into words, even in prayer. And I wonder what might begin to change when we stop pretending we can carry everything alone.
