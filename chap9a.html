# Chapter 9A ## Mamaw Anna: Until I Heard from Heaven Her name was Anna, but to us, she was Mamaw. That is what we called her, and that is how I will always remember her. Mamaw had a way of talking about prayer that stayed with me. When she did not know what to do, she would keep right on praying until she heard from heaven. She did not describe prayer as something she did only when life was going well. She prayed when she faced circumstances she could not figure out and when she did not know where the help she needed would come from. There were times when bills were due and there was no money to buy food. Those are the kinds of moments when worry can take over your thoughts. You know what needs to be paid, you know what your household needs, and you know that wishing the problem away will not make it disappear. When there is nothing obvious you can do to change the situation, it can be difficult to know what to say to God. Mamaw kept praying. There were also times when she was so sick that she could not find anyone to help her get to the hospital in the middle of the night. Imagine being ill, needing help, and not knowing who you can call or whether anyone will be able to come. In a moment like that, even forming a prayer can be difficult. You may be frightened, exhausted, or too unwell to explain what you are experiencing. There were moments when she struggled to understand what was happening to her. She could feel that something was wrong, yet she did not always know how to describe it or what to ask for. When you are sick and alone, the difference between needing medical help and being able to communicate that need can become frighteningly important. I cannot speak for every thought that passed through Mamaw's mind during those moments. But I remember what she taught us about continuing to pray when she did not know what to do. She would keep praying until she heard from heaven. ### When You Do Not Have the Words I think about Mamaw when I consider how many people believe they must know exactly what to say before they can come to God. We sometimes approach prayer as though we need to prepare the right words, identify the precise need, and understand our circumstances before we begin. But life does not always give us that kind of clarity. When the bills are due and there is no money for food, you may not know how the situation will change. When sickness comes in the middle of the night, you may not know how to explain what you are feeling. When fear and exhaustion take over, even the simplest request can feel difficult to express. Sometimes all you know is that you need help. Mamaw's example reminds me that prayer does not have to begin with a complete understanding of the problem. We can come before Jesus when our thoughts are unsettled and our words are incomplete. We can ask for help when we cannot see a solution, and we can continue seeking Him while taking whatever practical steps are available to us. Romans 8:26 speaks about our weakness and our inability to know what we ought to pray for. I find comfort in that truth because it acknowledges something we do not always want to admit: there are times when we simply do not know. Not knowing does not mean we have nothing left to say to God. We can tell Him that we are afraid. We can tell Him that we are sick, overwhelmed, or uncertain. We can ask Him for wisdom when we do not know what decision to make. We can also ask other people for help, because sometimes the assistance we need must come through someone who can respond to our circumstances. Faith does not require us to pretend that we are not in trouble. It allows us to be honest about the trouble we are facing. ### Until I Heard from Heaven What stays with me about Mamaw is the determination in her words. She would keep praying until she heard from heaven. That was how she described what she did when she did not know what to do. I do not want to turn her experience into a promise that every prayer will receive an immediate answer or that every difficult situation will resolve in the way we hope. Her story is not about pretending that financial hardship and sickness are easy to endure. It is about a woman who continued to turn toward God when she could not see a clear way forward. Sometimes we need to remember that distinction. Prayer is not a way of denying reality. It is a way of bringing our reality before the Lord. We can pray about the bills while also seeking practical assistance. We can pray when we are sick while also reaching out for medical care. We can ask God for wisdom while admitting that we do not understand what is happening. Trusting Him does not mean we must face every difficulty without the help of other people. And when we are too overwhelmed to find the right words, we can begin with the simplest truth we know: Jesus, I need You. Mamaw's life gave that truth a personal meaning for me. Her example reminds me that the moments when we do not know what to ask for can become the very moments when we learn to be honest about how much help we need. There are prayers that begin with confidence and prayers that begin with tears. There are prayers spoken in the quiet of a room and prayers whispered in the middle of a frightening night. There are prayers for needs we can explain and prayers for situations we barely understand ourselves. God does not require us to have every detail figured out before we come to Him. When I think of Mamaw Anna, I remember a woman who kept praying when she did not know what to do. Her words continue to remind me that I can keep seeking Jesus even when I cannot see the answer ahead of me. Sometimes the next step is not knowing what to ask. Sometimes it is simply refusing to stop reaching for God.
