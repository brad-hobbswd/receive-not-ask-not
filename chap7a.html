Chapter 7A When You Have Been Sick for So Long The Conversation with Jackie Jackie has been dealing with sickness for a long time. She has lost weight, asked others to pray for her, and continued looking for solutions. Although things have begun to improve, her journey has not been without further difficulties. Brad: Jackie, you have been sick for a long time. What has this season been like for you? Jackie: I have been sick for so long, and I have lost weight. I have been asking people to pray for me because I needed help and wanted to get better. Brad: Have things started to improve? Jackie: Yes, solutions started coming, and I began to get better. But now I am dealing with itching because the medication seems to be working against what my body needs. Brad: Through all of this, who do you put your trust in? Jackie: I love the Lord. He is the One I trust. Brad: I know church means a lot to you. What is it like when you cannot be there? Jackie: When I am not at church, I miss them. Jackie’s words were simple, but they revealed something important about her. Her love for the Lord and her connection to her church family remain central to her life, even while she continues dealing with her health. A Reflection There is something deeply personal about being sick for a long time. When your health begins to affect your weight, your strength, and your everyday life, it can become difficult to explain to others what you are going through. You may grow tired of feeling unwell, tired of searching for solutions, and tired of asking people to pray while still wondering when things will change. Jackie’s experience reminds me that improvement does not always mean the struggle is completely over. She began to see solutions and started feeling better, yet she has continued to face difficulties. Her story is still unfolding, and I do not want to write an ending that she has not reached. What stands out to me is her answer when I asked whom she trusts. She said that she loves the Lord and that He is the One she trusts. She did not need a long explanation to express where her confidence rests. Her circumstances have been difficult, but she has continued to speak about the Lord as the One she depends on. Jackie is nearly sixty five, and she has been dealing with sickness for a long time. I do not want to assume that her faith makes every difficult day easier or that trusting God means she never feels discouraged. What I do know is that she has continued asking for prayer, and she has been honest about needing help. Her love for church is another part of her story that deserves attention. She told me that when she cannot be there, she misses the people. That may sound like a small statement, but there is something meaningful about belonging to a church family whose presence you notice when you are away. Church is not simply a place to attend for Jackie. Her words show how much she values being there and being connected to the people she loves. When sickness keeps someone away from the place where they feel connected, the absence can be difficult. You can miss the worship, the fellowship, the familiar faces, and the simple experience of being present with others who share your faith. Sometimes people see someone missing from church and do not realize how much that person wishes they could be there. That is why I believe we need to remember those who are sick, not only when they first ask for prayer but throughout the long process of trying to get better. A message, a phone call, or a sincere prayer can remind someone that they have not been forgotten. We cannot always remove the burden, but we can make sure they do not have to feel forgotten while carrying it. James 5:16 reminds us to pray for one another. Prayer is not merely something we offer when we have no other response. It is one way we continue to carry each other through seasons when answers are slow and circumstances remain uncertain. Jackie’s story does not require me to explain why she became sick or promise how her recovery will unfold. It asks me to listen, recognize what she has endured, and remember that she is more than the illness she has been facing. She is a woman who loves the Lord, trusts Him, misses her church family when she cannot be there, and continues to need prayer while her story unfolds. Sometimes we hear a testimony after everything has changed. Other times, we hear it while the person is still waiting, still recovering, and still asking God for help. Jackie reminds me that we do not have to wait until every difficulty has passed before we acknowledge our trust in the Lord. We can love Him in the middle of uncertainty. We can ask others to pray while we continue seeking answers. We can be grateful for improvement without pretending that every struggle has ended. Her story is not finished, and neither is the conversation. For now, she has made one thing clear: she loves the Lord, and He is the One she trusts.
